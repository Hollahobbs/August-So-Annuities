The basics

What an annuity actually is

An annuity is a contract between you and an insurance company that shifts a portion of risk away from you and onto the company. Annuities can be both a boost to retirement savings and a dependable source of future income.

A contract built for your retirement, not the insurance company

Annuities help protect a portion of your nest egg from market volatility, safely setting aside funds for later expenses such as Medicare payments, long-term care planning, inheritance, and tax planning.

Provides guarantees for peace of mind

In retirement, annuities can offer pension-like cash flow for life, helping to supplement income like Social Security to meet needs and to protect against outliving savings if you live a long time.

Liquidity available for withdrawals

Annuities also offer flexibility around things like accessing principal or controlling the timing around taking distributions.

How it grows

Interest tied to an index — without risks, fees, or headaches

In a given crediting period, interest may be added to your account based on the movement of a market index, like the S&P 500.

In exchange for the downside protection, growth is typically limited by a cap or participation rate.

How is growth credited?

  • Bonus: Annuities can come with a bonus when you fund the annuity. Current bonus rates on annuities can be as high as 20–25% depending on carrier and contract length
  • Fixed rate: your account is credited with a fixed percentage rate for 1 year, based off of current interest rates, currently around 5–5.5%
  • Cap: your account is credited with the growth of the S&P 500 up to the cap
  • Participation rate: you receive uncapped growth of the S&P 500 at a certain percentage
  • Trigger: if the S&P 500 is positive for the year, you get a set percentage of growth — usually higher than a cap
  • In all three, if the S&P 500 is negative you receive 0% interest. Total safety.
How it protects

A floor under your account, even in a down year

If the linked index has a down year, most fixed indexed annuities credit $0 interest for that period rather than passing the loss on to your principal.

Gains already credited generally become part of your new baseline — so a future down year in the index typically doesn't erase the value you've already locked in.

Common questions

Before you book the call

Will I lose access to my money?

Most contracts allow penalty-free withdrawals up to a set percentage each year. Withdrawals beyond that amount — or large withdrawals during the surrender-charge period — can trigger surrender charges. Terms vary by carrier and contract, which is exactly what we'd review together.

Is this the same as investing in the stock market?

No. Your premium isn't directly invested in stocks. With a fixed indexed annuity, interest credited (if any) is based on a formula tied to an index's performance, subject to caps or participation rates set by the carrier.

What happens if the index goes down that year?

Typically, $0 interest is credited for that period rather than the account losing value due to market performance. Contract fees or optional rider costs, if elected, can still affect account value.

Are annuities insured like a bank account?

No. Annuities are not FDIC or NCUA insured. Guarantees are backed solely by the claims-paying ability and financial strength of the issuing insurance company — not by any bank or government agency.

Do I have to move all of my retirement savings?

No. Most people we talk with allocate a portion of their savings — money they specifically want protected — while keeping the rest invested elsewhere.
