The basics

What an annuity actually is

Strip away the sales language and it's a fairly simple idea: a contract between you and an insurance company, built around protection and, often, income.

A contract, not a stock

You put in a lump sum or series of payments. In exchange, the insurance company contractually agrees to specific protections and, if you choose, future income.

Not directly in the market

With a fixed indexed annuity, your premium isn't invested in stocks. Interest credited is instead linked to how an index performs — with protections built in.

Built for the long game

These are long-term contracts, often with a surrender-charge period. They're generally a fit for money you don't need to touch for several years.

How it grows

Interest tied to an index — without owning the index

In a given crediting period, interest may be added to your account based on the movement of a market index, like the S&P 500. You don't own shares and dividends aren't included.

In exchange for the downside protection, growth is typically limited by a cap, participation rate, or spread — so you generally receive a portion of the index's gain, not all of it.

  • Interest credited is typically locked in once applied
  • Caps and participation rates vary by carrier and product
  • Many contracts let you split premium across index strategies, a fixed-rate option, or a mix of both — and reallocate each contract year

How it protects

A floor under your account, even in a down year

If the linked index has a down year, most fixed indexed annuities credit $0 interest for that period rather than passing the loss on to your principal.

Gains already credited generally become part of your new baseline — so a future down year in the index typically doesn't erase the value you've already locked in.

  • Principal is shielded from market index losses
  • Optional riders can add guaranteed lifetime income
  • Contract fees or early withdrawals can still reduce value

Common questions

Before you book the call

Will I lose access to my money?

Most contracts allow penalty-free withdrawals up to a set percentage each year. Withdrawals beyond that amount — or large withdrawals during the surrender-charge period — can trigger surrender charges. Terms vary by carrier and contract, which is exactly what we'd review together.

Is this the same as investing in the stock market?

No. Your premium isn't directly invested in stocks. With a fixed indexed annuity, interest credited (if any) is based on a formula tied to an index's performance, subject to caps or participation rates set by the carrier.

What happens if the index goes down that year?

Typically, $0 interest is credited for that period rather than the account losing value due to market performance. Contract fees or optional rider costs, if elected, can still affect account value.

Are annuities insured like a bank account?

No. Annuities are not FDIC or NCUA insured. Guarantees are backed solely by the claims-paying ability and financial strength of the issuing insurance company — not by any bank or government agency.

Do I have to move all of my retirement savings?

No. Most people we talk with allocate a portion of their savings — money they specifically want protected — while keeping the rest invested elsewhere.
